beamSynth Synthetic beam patterns

Synthetic low and high beams that meet R149: start from a customer road-requirement JSON or from beam parameters, then review the iso-candela, road illumination and checks, and export an IES file.

Files are processed locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

The lobe limits keep the two light lobes inside a box, measured where each lobe falls to 5 % of its own peak. Without them the foreground lobe can spread to about ±90° and 40° down, which puts a bright patch just in front of the vehicle. At 0.65 m mounting height, 10° D lands about 3.7 m ahead, 6° D about 6.2 m and 4.5° D about 8.3 m. Tight limits can leave flux or V spread short of target; the result tiles mark a lobe held at a limit.

Q shapes the bottom of the beam, from a “+” shape at 0 to a strong smile at 1. Q does not move the hot spot or change the H spread. Default hot spot: 75R (75L in LHT) for Class C and V, 0.86D on V-V for symmetric classes, H-V for the high beam.

Criterion targets

Generates the lowest-flux beam that passes the R149 table and reaches every HSPR criterion target: a minimum for each range, width and flux value, a maximum for glare. The targets that come from a score give every criterion the same points, so a beam that meets them all scores at least that much; edit any of them to trade one criterion against another. Ratings follow GTB Recommended Practice SEP2023 for one function, with the lamp pair at the mounting height and separation set under Vehicle and road. Imax is not limited here; switch on Physical limits to cap it.

Paste a road-illuminate requirement set as is, or wrap it as "checks" with vehicle settings. Check types: point, range, width, zone, with op and limit; y is positive to the left, as in road-illuminate. Optional z per check sets its evaluation height. Optional class (B, CS, DS, ES; driving beam BS, DSES). Optional targets: imax, flux_lm, spreadH_deg, spreadV_deg. Optional roadFlux: target_lm, lamps (one or pair), zone {x1, x2, left, right}.

Vehicle
Physical limits

With an aperture given, Imax is held at or below LED luminance × aperture area, the most any optic can deliver. Efficiency converts the emitted flux into the LED flux needed. Leave the aperture blank for no limit.

Aim tolerance

At 0 the beam is solved for nominal aim only. Set a tolerance and the beam must pass ECE with the lamp mis-aimed anywhere in that box. A Class C beam can pass up to about ±0.3° H with ±0.15° V; beyond that the cut-off shoulder reaches the 75R point. Other classes pass ±0.4° H with ±0.2° V.

Flux on road

Luminous flux reaching the road surface inside this rectangle, measured from the lamps. Leave the minimum blank to get the least flux that meets the requirements; set it only when a minimum flux on road is required.

Cut-off geometry (low beam)

Elbow on the V-V line. G is the cut-off sharpness as R149 measures it: G = log E(β) − log E(β + 0.1°) on a vertical scan at 2.5°. The tool calibrates the cut-off so its steepest point sits on the elbow line. Symmetric classes use a flat cut-off, so shoulder angle and top are fixed.

Regulation test table

Entries: point (h, v), zone polygon, sum of point ids, or imax. Limits: min, max, minRatioImax, maxRef {id,k}. Always written for RHT; LHT is mirrored.
